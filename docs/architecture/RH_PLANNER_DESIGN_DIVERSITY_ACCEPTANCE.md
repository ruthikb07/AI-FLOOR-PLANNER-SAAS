## 4. What the engine now enforces

Seven mandatory checks reject a candidate outright — these are not scoring weights, and
`dominates` tests feasibility before any objective:

1. Exact tiling of the envelope — zero unassigned area, zero overlaps.
2. A staircase, a lift and common circulation present.
3. Common circulation forms **one connected component**.
4. Every stair and every lift adjoins circulation.
5. Every flat shares ≥ 1250 mm with circulation (a 1050 mm door plus returns).
6. Every flat has ≥ 1600 mm of exterior wall, on the building face or an open slot.
7. Every flat's span and run within the usable range for its unit type.

Checks 3, 4 and 6 were each added **after a test caught a real defect** in a family I had
written, and each would otherwise have shipped a plan that passed every area and overlap
check while being unbuildable:

- A courtyard plan whose two corridors sat on opposite sides of the void shared no wall,
  so flats served by one could never reach the other core.
- A double-loaded central core had the same split, fixed by reserving a cross-link past
  the end of the stack — you walk around the core, as in a real building.
- A single row of flats stacked through the middle of a deep plot left its inner flats
  with almost no exterior wall.

Three further bugs were caught the same way and are worth recording because none would
have been visible in an area or overlap check:

- **The `NORTH`/`SOUTH` frame maps were inverted**, so a north-entered flat silently
  received a south-entered layout. `localEastLandsOn` now asserts the invariant directly.
- **The required-core check averaged stair and lift boxes**, which read a genuinely
  central spine as off-centre. It now uses the core bounding region.
- **An absent lift lobby aliased the corridor** in the legacy single-box field, so the
  stilt and terrace sheets placed two rooms on one rectangle — a true 16.8 m² overlap.

## 6. Remaining limitations

**1. Low envelope utilization — the significant one.** Concepts use 35–62% of the
buildable envelope as dwelling area. The flat-size caps that fixed genuinely absurd room
proportions (an 85 m² bedroom beside a 21 m² bathroom, because the room slicer allocates
by ratio and was handed an oversized slab) leave real area unused.

The architecturally correct answer is to set the building in from the plot and hold the
margin as open space. `planFootprint` computes exactly that and is **deliberately
disabled**, with the reason recorded in the source: the canonical floor boundary is the
envelope, and `generateApartmentWalls` derives an exterior wall from a segment with one
owning room, so a balcony on a set-in face owns no exterior wall and
`validateApartmentClosure` rejects it with `BALCONY_EXTERIOR_EDGE_MISSING`. Expressing an
open margin properly needs it modelled as a floor **void** rather than as rooms, which
reaches into the wall generator, the area ledger and the structural grid. That is a
bounded piece of work, but it is not a small one, and guessing at it would have risked
the geometric integrity that is currently exact.

**2. Room proportions are still the legacy slicer's.** Living rooms remain
proportionally large relative to kitchens. `sliceFlatRooms` allocates by ratio and was
not rewritten, by design — the engine controls flat size and orientation, not the balance
of rooms within a flat.

**3. `SPLIT_CORE` and `PERIMETER_HABITABLE` are rarely selected** on the reference plot.
Both are feasible and exercised by the family tests; they rank below others there and
remain available on plots that suit them.

**4. No visual floor-plan images.** The assignment asked for rendered 2D plan artifacts.
The canonical geometry, room schedules and fingerprints for all three concepts are
written to `docs/architecture/concepts/`, and `backend` CAD/PDF export is green against
this model, but no plan images were rendered and compared visually in this session. The
geometric differences are evidenced numerically, not pictorially.

**5. Diversity thresholds are calibrated on one plot.** The adjacency, core-shift,
entrance-shift and zoning thresholds were calibrated against the 40 × 50 m and 18 × 25 m
fixtures. They are not validated across the full range of plot shapes.
