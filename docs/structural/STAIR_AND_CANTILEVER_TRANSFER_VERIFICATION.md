# Stair and cantilever gravity transfer

The distributed cantilever benchmark is a horizontal 4 m by 1 m elastic strip under 2 kN/m² pressure, clamped across the full root. Both the slab-only and the concentric two-beam variants deliver 8 kN vertical reaction and −16 kN·m about the root. The 32-quad coupled variant returns −11.019450 mm tip displacement and 0.003670053 rad rotation. Its closed-form displacement is −10.989011 mm. This evidence covers the tested clamped geometry and idealized shared DOFs. [Benchmark results and tolerances](DISTRIBUTED_GRAVITY_BENCHMARKS.md)

The synthetic waist strip is 1 m wide and 160 mm thick with a 4 m plan run and 3 m rise per span. A global vertical load of 4 kN/m of sloping length produces 10 and 10 kN in the simply supported case. The continuous case returns 7.500360, 24.999280 and 7.500360 kN. Both match the analytical reactions and global first moments within 10⁻⁶ kN and 10⁻⁶ kN·m. The continuous reference uses the force method with the middle bearing as redundant and projects both bending and axial flexibility onto the vertical bearing direction. Both solves took about 62 ms in the recorded Windows run.

The archives contain registered floor landings, intermediate landings and flight outlines. Bearing region, inclination, support member identity, connection elevation and restraint review remain required for actual stair force and moment transfer. The balcony and terrace cantilever checks also require reviewed root stiffness, actual offsets, torsion and the continuity of the adjoining deck. No nearest-beam transfer is applied by the mesher. ## Reviewed straight stair transfer

The production input panel and load assembler support a simply supported straight flight bearing on two physical beam lines. The review identifies the physical waist and the lower and upper beam IDs, architecture digest, upper landing elevation, waist thickness, required bearing width, imposed load and reference. Both beam lines must carry the entire flight width at the correct level and plan position, with contiguous analytical children and compatible flight direction. When the operator supplies no review, modeled waist/step DL, finish SDL and imposed LL remain in the unassigned ledger. A proximity candidate cannot receive those loads. Intermediate landings and multi-flight frames still need separate physical modelling.

The synthetic two-level frame test uses a 4 m horizontal run, 2.8 m rise, 2.5 m width, 160 mm waist and 175 mm risers. It integrates sloped concrete volume and step volume independently, compares transferred loads for each case, and checks the change from the missing-input ledger. The lower and upper bearings each receive half the total uniform gravity. This verifies load transfer to the identified beams. Foundation acceptance requires analysis of those reactions.

A full canonical stair fixture also verifies physical beam IDs in the saved physical model, the RCC strip demand under the reviewed live load and the bearing reference in IFC. The simply supported flight uses the uniform-load moment wL²/8. Missing bearing review and unverified shear or anchorage block overall stair design acceptance.
