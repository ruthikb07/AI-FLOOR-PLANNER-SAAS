# Parametric core verification

The wizard exposes independent controls for preferred core arrangement, exact stair and lift counts, internal open-to-sky spaces, exterior living and wet-wall coordination. `architectureOptions.coreDimensions` accepts whole-millimetre project dimensions for stair flight width, maximum riser, tread, landing, lift shaft, circulation and court. A wet-wall request alone leaves core selection automatic. Exact counts and OTS constraints apply to the generated canonical model even when the preferred split organization cannot fit.

The opposed stair/lift topology sizes the stair bay from the largest modeled riser interval, two flights, tread run, floor and intermediate landings, and the wall allowance. The central court adapts within the available plate unless the brief supplies a dimension. Explicit passage width is measured at successive cross-sections with actual parallel boundary-wall thickness and axis-aligned column obstructions. The first taller fixture reproduced 2,160 mm of passage against a requested 2,200 mm. Its proposed centerline cell had allowed 150 mm for two walls. The sizing routine now allows up to two half-width 230 mm boundary walls. Hosted openings and the connected common route are checked independently of the width calculation.

The default four-flat courtyard, a taller larger plan with specified dimensions, and a single-stair, single-lift plan with internal OTS prohibited are covered in the production HTTP regressions. The feasibility search can decline the split organization on a tight site, incompatible count, or conflicting mandatory brief. Curved and obstructed general egress analysis remains outside this measurement.

The focused HTTP replay accepted both the original 38 × 32 m two-residential-storey split layout and a 44 × 37 m split layout with 3,600 mm storey rise. The second fixture carries 2,300 × 2,400 mm lift shafts, a 3,600 mm court, at least 2,200 mm clear circulation and the requested flight, tread and riser limits. The separate one-stair/one-lift brief succeeded with an explicit no-OTS constraint despite its split-court preference. The wizard, CAD preview, IFC export and Tower checks were exercised together for the four-flat study.


A separate 45 × 27 m production probe accepted the split plan. The 24 × 32.5 m two-flat and 20 × 22 m four-flat probes used fallback organizations with the requested counts and internal OTS. The first 52 × 42 m six-flat probe refused a candidate whose lift count fell below the requested two. The final gate refused publication, and topology now screens explicit two-lift requirements before ranking candidates.
